Startup Cash Runway, Gross vs. Net Burn, and Reaching Default Alive

Gross Burn, Net Burn, and the Question Every Investor Asks First

Paul Graham popularized one of the sharpest diagnostic questions in startup finance: "Assuming your expenses remain constant and your revenue growth continues at its current pace, will you reach profitability before your bank balance hits zero?" If the answer is yes, your company is Default Alive—you control your destiny and raise venture capital from a position of leverage. If the answer is no, you are Default Dead—and every month you delay either raising funds or cutting burn narrows your survival options.

Founders routinely overstate their runway by dividing cash by net burn while forgetting upcoming payroll tax remittances, annual SaaS renewals, or optimistic growth assumptions that fail to materialize.

Core Burn Rate and Runway Formulas

Gross Monthly Burn = Total Monthly Cash Operating Outflows (Payroll + Cloud + Rent + Marketing)
Net Monthly Burn = Gross Monthly Burn − Monthly Cash Revenue Collected (MRR)
Zero-Growth Runway (Months) = Cash Balance ÷ Net Monthly Burn
Burn Multiple = Net Burn ÷ Net New ARR Added (Quarterly or Monthly)

Always model both Zero-Growth Runway (worst-case floor if sales stall tomorrow) and Growth-Adjusted Runway (iterating monthly cash balance as MRR compounds).

Worked Simulation: $650,000 Cash, $62,000 Gross Burn, $24,000 MRR

Consider a seed-stage B2B startup with $650,000 in the bank, $62,000/month in fixed gross burn, and $24,000/month in recurring revenue (Net Burn = $38,000/month):

Practical Runway Extension Playbook When Default Dead

  • Audit Fixed Cloud & SaaS Sprawl First: Unused staging clusters, over-provisioned database tiers, and redundant analytics seats routinely account for 8%–15% of non-payroll burn and can be trimmed in a single afternoon without hurting product velocity.
  • Shift to Annual Prepayment Incentives: Offering existing monthly customers two months free for switching to annual billing brings 10 months of cash into the bank immediately, slashing net burn while simultaneously improving retention.
  • Freeze Speculative Paid Acquisition: Pause any marketing channel whose CAC payback exceeds 9 months until your organic pipeline or core sales motion brings Burn Multiple back under 1.5x.

The 6-Month Fundraising Rule

Institutional fundraising rounds take 3 to 6 months from first warm intro to wire transfer. If your Zero-Growth Runway drops to 9 months, you effectively have 3 months left to either launch a raise or execute a decisive burn reduction—never wait until 3 months of cash remain, when term sheets evaporate or turn punitive.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I use booked ARR or actual cash collected when computing Net Burn?

Always use actual cash collected in your bank account. An enterprise customer on Net-60 terms or a delayed wire does not pay Friday's payroll.

What is a healthy Burn Multiple for a seed or Series A SaaS startup?

A Burn Multiple under 1.0x is exceptional efficiency, 1.0x to 1.5x is strong, 1.5x to 2.0x is acceptable during deliberate product buildout, and above 2.5x signals you are burning too much cash per dollar of new ARR.

Does switching customers from monthly to annual billing extend runway?

Yes—dramatically. Collecting 12 months of subscription cash upfront (even with a 15%–20% annual discount) acts as non-dilutive working capital that immediately shrinks net cash burn.

How should I account for planned hires in a runway model?

Add fully loaded payroll cost—typically 1.20x to 1.30x base salary to include employer payroll taxes, health benefits, and equipment—starting in the exact month each hire joins.
