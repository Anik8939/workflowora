One of the most expensive pricing errors in retail, e-commerce, and contracting is confusing Markup % with Gross Profit Margin %—or assuming a 50% gross margin guarantees a healthy bottom line before factoring in overhead and customer acquisition.

1. The Three Profit Equations Every Founder Must Know

1. Gross Profit Margin % = ((Revenue − COGS) ÷ Revenue) × 100
2. Markup % = ((Revenue − COGS) ÷ COGS) × 100
3. Net Profit Margin % = ((Revenue − COGS − Operating Expenses − Ads − Taxes) ÷ Revenue) × 100

Notice the denominator: Margin always divides profit by the selling price (Revenue), whereas Markup divides profit by the unit cost (COGS). If an item costs you $100 and you add a 50% markup to sell it for $150, your Gross Profit is $50—which is only a 33.3% Gross Margin ($50 ÷ $150), not 50%!

3. Healthy Margin Benchmarks by Industry

  • DTC E-Commerce & Consumer Goods: 60%–75% Gross Margin / 10%–20% Net Profit Margin (needed to absorb paid social CAC).
  • SaaS & Software Products: 75%–88% Gross Margin / 15%–30% Net Profit Margin at scale.
  • Freelance & Digital Agencies: 50%–65% Delivery Gross Margin / 20%–35% Net Operating Margin.

Common Calculation Mistakes That Cost Thousands (And How to Fix Them)

When auditing real-world spreadsheets across startups, freelancers, and household budgets, the same three mathematical blind spots appear repeatedly:

  1. Ignoring Hidden Variable Costs & Fees: Whether you are calculating e-commerce ROAS, SaaS CAC, or a mortgage refinance, omitting payment processing fees (2.9% + $0.30), shipping fulfillment, software overhead, or loan origination charges inflates your projected return by 12% to 25%. Always enter fully loaded costs into the calculator above.
  2. Confusing Simple Averages with Compounded Rates: A 2% monthly churn rate or a 22% credit card APR compounds every period. Over 12 to 36 months, ignoring compounding frequency creates a massive gap between your back-of-the-napkin estimate and your actual bank balance.
  3. Optimizing a Single Metric in Isolation: Lowering Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) by attracting low-intent bargain hunters often spikes 60-day churn and destroys Lifetime Value (LTV). Similarly, choosing a 7-year loan over a 3-year loan lowers your monthly EMI by 35% but nearly doubles total interest paid. Always evaluate the ratio and total lifecycle cost together.

3. Should I use gross revenue or gross profit margin when calculating ratios like LTV:CAC or ROAS? ▼

Always use Gross Profit Margin (after subtracting Cost of Goods Sold, shipping, fulfillment, and payment processing fees). Calculating LTV or Break-Even ROAS on raw top-line revenue overstates your true spending power and can lead to unprofitable ad scaling.
