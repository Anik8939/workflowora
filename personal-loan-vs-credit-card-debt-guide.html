Personal Loan vs. Credit Card Debt: Which Should You Pay Off First?

Carrying a revolving balance on a credit card at 20% to 28% APR is one of the most expensive financial traps. Understanding the structural difference between revolving credit and an installment loan helps you eliminate debt years faster.

1. Revolving Interest vs. Fixed Installment EMI

Credit cards compound interest daily on your average daily balance and only require a 2% to 3% minimum payment—keeping you in debt for over a decade. By contrast, a fixed personal loan locks in a lower APR (typically 7% to 12%) with a guaranteed payoff date over 24 to 60 months.

2. Debt Avalanche vs. Debt Snowball Method

  • Debt Avalanche (Mathematically Optimal): Pay minimum payments on all debts, and direct every extra dollar toward the account with the highest APR.
  • Debt Snowball (Behavioral Momentum): Pay off your smallest balance first to score quick psychological wins, then roll that payment into the next debt.
