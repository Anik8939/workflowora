Over 54% of freelancers and small B2B agencies experience late client payments at least once a quarter. Writing vague terms like "Due upon receipt" without a concrete calendar due date or late-fee clause puts your invoice at the very bottom of a corporate Accounts Payable queue.

1. Standard Invoice Payment Terms Decoded

  • Net 7 / Net 15: Payment is due in full within 7 or 15 calendar days of the invoice issue date. Best for independent freelancers, designers, and agencies.
  • Net 30: The standard corporate accounting window (30 calendar days from invoice date).
  • 2/10 Net 30: The client receives a 2% discount if they pay within 10 days; otherwise, the full balance is due in 30 days. Because earning 2% for paying 20 days early equals a 36.7% annualized return for the client's CFO, enterprise accounting teams almost always pay 2/10 Net 30 invoices immediately!

2. How to Calculate Late Payment Interest on Overdue Invoices

In the US, standard commercial contracts specify a late fee of 1.5% per month (18% APR) prorated daily once an invoice passes its due date:

Overdue Invoice Late Fee Formula:
Daily Late Interest = Invoice Balance × (Annual Late APR ÷ 365)
Total Late Fee = Daily Late Interest × Number of Days Past Due
Example: A $4,000 invoice that is 45 days overdue at 18% APR (1.5%/mo) accrues $88.77 in late interest ($4,000 × 0.18 ÷ 365 × 45).

3. Copy-Paste Late Fee Clause for Your Invoice Footer

Include this exact line in the Notes section of your invoice before starting work: "Payment is due within 15 days of invoice date (Net 15). Balances unpaid after the due date are subject to a 1.5% monthly late finance charge (18% APR) prorated daily, and work in progress may be paused until the account is brought current."

Common Calculation Mistakes That Cost Thousands (And How to Fix Them)

When auditing real-world spreadsheets across startups, freelancers, and household budgets, the same three mathematical blind spots appear repeatedly:

  1. Ignoring Hidden Variable Costs & Fees: Whether you are calculating e-commerce ROAS, SaaS CAC, or a mortgage refinance, omitting payment processing fees (2.9% + $0.30), shipping fulfillment, software overhead, or loan origination charges inflates your projected return by 12% to 25%. Always enter fully loaded costs into the calculator above.
  2. Confusing Simple Averages with Compounded Rates: A 2% monthly churn rate or a 22% credit card APR compounds every period. Over 12 to 36 months, ignoring compounding frequency creates a massive gap between your back-of-the-napkin estimate and your actual bank balance.
  3. Optimizing a Single Metric in Isolation: Lowering Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) by attracting low-intent bargain hunters often spikes 60-day churn and destroys Lifetime Value (LTV). Similarly, choosing a 7-year loan over a 3-year loan lowers your monthly EMI by 35% but nearly doubles total interest paid. Always evaluate the ratio and total lifecycle cost together.

3. Should I use gross revenue or gross profit margin when calculating ratios like LTV:CAC or ROAS? ▼

Always use Gross Profit Margin (after subtracting Cost of Goods Sold, shipping, fulfillment, and payment processing fees). Calculating LTV or Break-Even ROAS on raw top-line revenue overstates your true spending power and can lead to unprofitable ad scaling.
