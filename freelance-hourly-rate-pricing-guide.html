How to Price Your Freelance Services Without Underselling Yourself

The biggest mistake new freelancers, designers, developers, and consultants make is dividing their desired annual salary by 2,000 hours (40 hours × 50 weeks). When you work for yourself, not every working hour is a billable hour—and you must cover your own software, equipment, unpaid leave, and taxes.

1. The 25-Hour Billable Week Reality

In a standard 40-hour workweek, solo operators spend roughly 35% to 40% of their time on non-billable business tasks:

  • Client Acquisition & Proposals (6 hrs/week): Discovery calls, writing scopes of work, and following up on leads.
  • Admin, Invoicing & Bookkeeping (4 hrs/week): Sending invoices, tracking expenses, and managing project boards.
  • Skill Upgrades & Portfolio Updates (5 hrs/week): Learning new AI workflows and refining your craft.

That leaves approximately 25 true billable hours per week across 48 working weeks per year (1,200 billable hours/year).

2. The True Freelance Floor Rate Formula

Minimum Hourly Rate = (Target Net Income + Annual Business Overhead + Taxes) ÷ (Billable Hours/Week × Working Weeks)

For example, if you want to take home $48,000/year and have $4,000/year in software, internet, and hardware costs ($52,000 total) across 1,200 billable hours, your minimum floor rate is $44/hour, or $264 per 6-hour day.

3. When to Switch from Hourly Billing to Fixed Day Rates

As you use AI tools and templates to work faster, pure hourly billing punishes your speed. Quote clients a Full Day Rate (6 focused hours) or a fixed milestone price so you are rewarded for delivering fast, high-quality outcomes.
