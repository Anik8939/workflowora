The Power of Compound Interest: How $300/Month Builds Long-Term Wealth

Compound interest is the financial mechanism where the returns you earn on your savings or investments begin earning returns of their own. While linear saving only grows by what you deposit out of pocket, compounding creates an exponential growth curve over 10, 20, and 30 years.

1. The Rule of 72: How Fast Does Your Money Double?

Before running complex spreadsheets, financial planners use a mental shortcut called the Rule of 72. Simply divide 72 by your expected annual rate of return to see how many years it takes for a lump sum to double:

  • At a 6% annual return: 72 ÷ 6 = 12 years to double your money.
  • At an 8% annual return: 72 ÷ 8 = 9 years to double your money.
  • At a 10% annual return: 72 ÷ 10 = 7.2 years to double your money.

2. Real-World Growth Table: $5,000 Initial + $300/Month at 8%

See what happens when you start with $5,000 and consistently invest $300 every month at an average 8% annual return compounded monthly:

Time Horizon Your Total Deposits Compound Profit Earned Total Portfolio Value
5 Years $23,000 +$6,487 $29,487
10 Years $41,000 +$24,678 $65,678
20 Years $77,000 +$124,398 $201,398

3. Three Keys to Maximizing Compound Growth

Notice how by Year 20, your compound profit ($124,398) is significantly larger than the total cash you deposited out of pocket ($77,000). To maximize this effect, automate your monthly contributions on payday, keep investment management fees below 0.20%, and avoid interrupting the compounding curve early.
