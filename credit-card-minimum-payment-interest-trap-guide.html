The Credit Card Minimum Payment Trap: How Daily Compounding APR Keeps You in Debt

When you carry a $10,000 balance on a credit card at 22% APR, your statement might only ask for a $200 to $280 minimum payment. It feels manageable—until you run the amortization math and discover that paying only the minimum will keep you in debt for over 24 years and cost more than $16,000 in interest alone.

1. How Banks Calculate Your Credit Card Interest Daily

Unlike fixed personal loans or mortgages that calculate interest once a month on a declining schedule, credit cards charge interest using a Daily Periodic Rate (DPR) applied to your Average Daily Balance:

Daily Periodic Rate (DPR) = Annual APR ÷ 365
Monthly Interest Charge = Average Daily Balance × DPR × Days in Billing Cycle (30 or 31)
Example on $10,000 at 22% APR: Daily Rate = 0.06027%/day → $6.03/day in interest ($180.82/month)!

If your minimum payment is $200 and $180.82 goes straight to interest, only $19.18 actually reduces your $10,000 debt in Month 1.

3. Debt Avalanche vs. Debt Snowball: Which Wins?

  • Debt Avalanche (Mathematically Optimal): Pay minimums on all cards, and throw 100% of extra cash at the card with the highest APR first. This saves the maximum total dollars in interest.
  • Debt Snowball (Behavioral Momentum): Pay off the smallest dollar balance first regardless of interest rate to eliminate an entire monthly bill quickly and free up DTI cash flow.
