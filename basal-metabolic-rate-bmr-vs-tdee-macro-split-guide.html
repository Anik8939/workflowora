BMR vs. TDEE Explained: How to Calculate Your Calorie Deficit & Protein Macros Without Crash Dieting

One of the most common fitness mistakes is eating below your BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) when trying to lose weight—which triggers muscle loss, fatigue, and metabolic slowdown. Sustainable fat loss or lean muscle gain starts with knowing the exact mathematical gap between your BMR and your TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure).

1. The Mifflin-St Jeor Equation for BMR & TDEE

Clinical nutritionists rely on the Mifflin-St Jeor Formula to estimate resting Basal Metabolic Rate (the calories your organs burn in 24 hours of complete rest):

Men BMR = (10 × Weight in kg) + (6.25 × Height in cm) − (5 × Age in years) + 5
Women BMR = (10 × Weight in kg) + (6.25 × Height in cm) − (5 × Age in years) − 161
TDEE (Maintenance Calories) = BMR × Activity Multiplier (1.2 to 1.9)

3. The 3,500-Calorie Rule & Macro Math

One pound of body fat stores roughly 3,500 calories of energy. Eating 500 calories below your TDEE each day creates a 3,500-calorie weekly deficit—burning 1.0 lb (0.45 kg) of pure fat per week while preserving lean muscle mass when paired with 1.6g to 2.0g of protein per kilogram of body weight (where Protein = 4 kcal/g, Carbs = 4 kcal/g, and Fats = 9 kcal/g).
